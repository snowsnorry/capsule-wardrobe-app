# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is a personal wardrobe planner using the app to generate, inspect, refine, and save capsule wardrobes. They work through preference-driven flows: gender or audience, style, season, occasions, accent color, pattern, and additional free-text constraints. They also use the product explorer and statistics screens to understand the clothing database behind the generated capsules.

## Product Purpose

Capsule Wardrobe App generates capsule wardrobes from explicit user preferences, then lets users review items, regenerate parts of the wardrobe, save capsule versions, export outfit sets, explore product data, and inspect aggregate product statistics. Success means the user can move from preference setup to a credible, editable capsule with minimal friction and enough product evidence to trust the result.

## Brand Personality

Practical, curated, calm. The app should feel like a focused wardrobe planning workspace: clear enough for repeated use, warm enough to suit a personal styling context, and restrained enough that clothing images and filter choices remain the center of attention.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product feel like a generic SaaS dashboard. Do not make it feel like a generic AI slop app. Avoid decorative AI tropes, vague magic-language presentation, noisy gradients, overproduced marketing sections, and analytics chrome that overwhelms the wardrobe task.

## Design Principles

1. Wardrobe output leads. Clothing images, capsule names, selected preferences, and product attributes must carry the interface before decoration does.
2. Preferences stay legible. Filters are the product's control surface, so chips, fields, and sidebars must remain scannable, predictable, and easy to revise.
3. Trust over spectacle. AI generation should appear as a normal workflow state with clear actions, progress, partial regeneration, save/revert, and export affordances.
4. Data supports styling. Search and statistics should clarify product coverage without turning the app into a generic analytics dashboard.
5. Warm restraint wins. The interface may feel personal and curated, but it must keep the density and consistency expected from a product tool.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target accessible product UI basics: keyboard-reachable controls, visible focus states inherited from MUI, readable contrast for text and selected states, responsive layouts for mobile and desktop, and reduced-motion support for collapsible navigation where motion is used. Color should never be the only indicator of selection, unsaved state, or errors.
